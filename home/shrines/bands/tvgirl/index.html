TV Girl is one of my favourite bands ever! As you can tell by this page, my favourite album of their discography is "Who Really Cares". The first track I've discovered from this album is Not Allowed—but if you ask me my favourite track from the whole album, there would be some tough competition between Not Allowed and Cigarettes out the Window, with Taking What's Not Yours and Loving Machine shortly behind as well. There's just something else about the beginning and the ending of Cigarettes out the Window and Not Allowed respectively. Everytime I listen to it, it seems as if I get transported into another world, one without any responsibilities, somewhere secluded, like in the forest, or deep in space. A place unknown yet close to my heart, someone nobody else will understand. Kind of like the feeling you have staring out a train window, and it's all just fog and trees. You think about how it'll feel to leave, alone, trekking through the dirt, leaving your old life behind. It's honestly fascinating how simple melodies can evoke this sense of nostalgia, longing and solitude at once. You can call me dramatic, but ultimately this album has followed me through some of the most interesting times of my life, so it's very near and dear to me, I must say.

Melodic qualities aside, I do realise there has been controversies surrounding the lyrics and themes of this album. But to that I say, what DID you expect? There is a saying that many of you have heard, "Art is meant to disturb the comforted and comfort the disturbed." I feel like this is one great example. This album explores hookup culture, substance abuse, misogyny, narcissism... If you expected the lyrics to be happy too, I'm not sure you're ready to handle these heavy-loaded topics with the understanding it deserves. I get that people believe that this album / this band does have misogynistic undertones, because, well, while yes, the band DOES only consist of men, despite being called TV Girl... putting that aside, if you read the lyrics (and between them), in my interpretation, they're not really 'condemning' women for these acts, per se.

Unfortunately this artist is one of the contributors to me being moody half of the time (have you seen that video about listening to TV Girl before giving the day a chance?), but this doesn't stop the album bringing banger after banger.

They actually went to my city to perform once, but I wasn't able to watch... huhuhu...

"An album about sex or lack thereof, and its consequences or lack thereof."
